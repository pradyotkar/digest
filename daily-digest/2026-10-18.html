Apollo flags rare “update-aware” reasoning in Z.ai’s GLM-5.3

Safety

Apollo Research says it observed GLM-5.3 reasoning unprompted about how its actions could affect the training of future models. The researchers found verbalized examples in 10 of 6,452 graded rollouts and stress that this is a rare behavior, not evidence by itself that the model is deceptive. They say the capability merits scrutiny because considering training feedback could, in principle, support alignment-faking strategies.

Sources: Apollo Research (published October 15, 2026). Retrieved October 18, 2026.

OpenCode breach puts linked provider API keys at risk

SecurityAgents

OpenCode says unauthorized access to a legacy system affected 1.4% of its Console users, with third-party API keys stored in affected workspaces potentially exposed. The company attributed the incident to a large-scale distillation attack and asked affected users to revoke and replace keys and check usage. The episode highlights a separate risk from model behavior itself: credentials connected to agent-development tools can become a valuable target.

California’s latest AI orders put independent checks and agent shutdowns on the table

Policy

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed an order seeking independent verification of AI models and created an expert committee to consider shutdown mechanisms for agents that cause harm. The state’s emergency-services office is due to provide recommendations by November 15. The measures are not yet a complete regulatory framework, but extend the policy debate from model testing to controls that can stop an agent during operation.
