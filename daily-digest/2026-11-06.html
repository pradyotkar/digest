OpenAI launches GPT-6 Cyber, the model behind a $1.2 million autonomous Linux bug hunt

TOP STORYModel ReleaseSecurity

On November 5 OpenAI announced GPT-6 Cyber, a model aimed at security research and offered first to select "Daybreak Red" enterprise customers, including teams at Mozilla, Palo Alto Networks and Cisco. Fortune reports the launch lands amid growing alarm about AI-driven cyber attacks, with OpenAI pairing the capability release with stronger alignment claims.

The model powered a headline result disclosed the same week: an autonomous pipeline that scanned roughly 2 million lines of Linux kernel code and found, validated and patched 948 local privilege-escalation bugs in a single run—at $1,213,430 in API credits, or about $1,279 per unique flaw. The economics matter as much as the count: large-scale security research is becoming a billable, automatable commodity, and defenders and attackers will race to use it first.

Election rebuke: data-center restrictions passed in nearly every ballot measure as AI stocks sold off

AI PolicyInfrastructure

Midterm results delivered the sharpest political verdict yet on the AI buildout. CNBC counted 41 data-center ballot measures across 10 states; the Associated Press reports restrictions were approved in 44 of 45 completed local referendums, with Ohio alone passing bans or curbs in at least 14 municipalities. Fortune calls the state the prototype for how American voters respond to mass AI infrastructure expansion.

The backlash is now structural: Bloomberg estimates some 40% of planned U.S. data-center capacity sits in states that just elected governors who campaigned to pause or stop construction, and fears of an AI crackdown contributed to a roughly $2 trillion rout in tech stocks (NPR). OpenAI responded with a community-transparency framework—pledging to drop NDAs in data-center deals with public officials and pay for local power costs (CNBC).

Anthropic rolls out Claude Personal Finance, linking real bank accounts via Plaid

OpenAI ships ChatGPT Images 2.6 with native GIFs—and two new API models

ProductAPI

Cognition cuts coding-agent prices to a fifth with SWE-2 Flash

PricingAI Agents

Cognition, maker of Devin, released SWE-2 Flash on November 4: a 276B-parameter (12B active) mixture-of-experts coding model post-trained from Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling-Small, running at three times the speed and one-fifth the price of its SWE-2 flagship. On benchmarks it edges SWE-2 on FrontierCode 2.0 (24.0 vs 23.8) but trails on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (20.0 vs 27.3)—a reminder that cheap-and-fast agents still trade capability for throughput.

Meanwhile Thinking Machines itself shipped Inkling 2 Small (developer access via Tinker, weights promised after safety testing), and Baidu released its 280B-parameter ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base as open source. The agent supply chain is maturing fast: frontier labs build bases, agent companies post-train them, and price competition arrives a release later.
