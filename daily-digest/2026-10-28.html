Anthropic study finds analysts often follow an AI agent’s recommendation

Human OversightResearch

Anthropic reported a study in which 50 professional statistical analysts used Claude Code to assess a farming cooperative and make a recommendation. Analysts followed the agent’s preferred direction 86% of the time across four analytical choices—well above the 50% expected if the recommendation made no difference.

The strongest caution came when the agent disagreed with an analyst’s previously stated view: participants switched away from their own position 60% of the time, compared with 6% when the agent agreed. The result is a small, task-specific study, not a universal measure of AI influence, but it underscores why decision processes need independent review rather than treating confident model output as a second opinion by default.

Amazon pushes back on token-count productivity metrics

Enterprise AIProductivity

An internal Amazon memo made public Tuesday urged software engineers to stop “tokenmaxxing” and use AI more judiciously. Amazon called token consumption and code generation “deeply flawed” measures of productivity, arguing that teams should be evaluated on long-term outcomes instead.

The message reflects a more mature phase of workplace AI adoption: maximizing generated output is not the same as delivering better software. Quality, maintenance burden, security and time-to-value are more meaningful signals than how much text a model produces.
