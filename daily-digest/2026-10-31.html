Four AI companies back a shared website opt-out signal for model training

TOP STORYPolicy & SafetyWeb Publishing

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic say they will support a common “policy token” that publishers can place in robots.txt to express AI-training preferences across providers, rather than maintaining separate rules for each crawler. Google says it plans to support the control in 2027; OpenAI says implementation is expected in the coming months, while The Verge reports Anthropic has already adopted it.

This is a voluntary shared signal, not a new binding standard or a guarantee that every crawler will honor it immediately. Google says existing provider-specific controls remain available, so site owners can combine the broad preference with more granular choices.

Source: The Verge — “The biggest names in AI are backing a new way to opt out of scraping” (published October 30, 2026); Google Search Central — “Supporting a shared AI training control for websites” (published October 30, 2026). Retrieved October 31, 2026.

OpenAI revises its Model Spec with clearer principles for capable, agentic systems

Policy & SafetyModel Behavior

OpenAI’s October 29 Model Spec revision organizes intended behavior around five values: faithfulness to human intent, harm minimization, honesty and transparency, human agency, and care. The update adds guidance on context and memory, personalization, oversight, and how models should control and communicate side effects.

The document is a public description of intended behavior—not evidence that deployed systems fully satisfy each principle. Its emphasis on authorization, oversight and communicating actions is especially relevant as models gain more ability to use tools and act beyond chat.

Source: OpenAI Help Center — “Model Release Notes” (Model Spec update, October 29, 2026). Retrieved October 31, 2026.

Mistral publishes open weights for its 1-trillion-parameter Large 4 model

Model ReleaseOpen WeightsPricing

Mistral’s Large 4, nicknamed “Le Chonk,” is a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 1 trillion total parameters and 52 billion active per token, according to the company. Mistral announced open weights under a modified MIT-style license and lists API pricing at $0.70 per million input tokens.

The headline parameter count is not the same as the compute used for each token, and reported performance benchmarks are company claims. Mistral also said it withheld a security-focused variant for contractual vetting after seeing potential for misuse, underscoring the tension between open access and risk controls.

Source: Mistral AI — “Mistral Large 4 (Le Chonk) is now open weight” (published October 29, 2026, as reported in the October 31 roundup). Retrieved October 31, 2026.

DoorDash says third-party AI agents have placed only several hundred orders

AI AgentsApplications

DoorDash CEO Tony Xu told analysts that third-party agents have generated “several hundred” orders in total so far. TechCrunch contrasts that with roughly three million daily deliveries reported by the company for 2025. DoorDash says it is open to learning how to work with agent makers, even as most customers continue ordering in its own app.

The gap is a useful reality check on claims that agents are already transforming consumer commerce: integration and access remain uneven, and early activity is tiny beside established channels. Adoption and the economics of referrals or advertising will be worth watching.

Source: TechCrunch — “DoorDash said it’s only received ‘several hundred’ orders from AI agents” (published October 30, 2026). Retrieved October 31, 2026.

Anthropic expands its AI-for-science program to 20 research partners

ApplicationsResearch

Anthropic highlighted 20 scientific foundations, institutions and research groups joining an expansion of its AI for Science program, which provides Claude credits for researchers working on high-impact projects. The initiative extends the application of general-purpose models into research settings through institutional partnerships rather than a standalone model launch.

The announcement signals continued investment in science workflows, but credits and partnerships alone do not establish scientific impact. The meaningful measures will be reproducible results, time saved, and whether researchers can validate model contributions.

Source: Anthropic — “AI for Science expands to foundations and research institutions” (published October 30, 2026). Retrieved October 31, 2026.
