Personal Agent Protocol Aims to Standardize How Agents Act for Consumers

Agent EcosystemStandards

Sierra and Meta announced the Personal Agent Protocol, an OAuth-based proposal for personal agents to authenticate with businesses and carry context across channels. The described design gives consumers a way to distinguish read-only from write access and lets companies define permitted actions.

Walmart, Shopify, Stripe, Rocket and Genesys are named as founding partners. A common protocol could reduce reliance on agents scraping websites, while making authorization and responsibility more explicit; the specification is still planned rather than finalized.

Fujitsu Starts Trials of Four AI Agents for Retail Operations

AgentsApplication

Fujitsu says its trial environment covers agents for sales analysis, customer loyalty, merchandise planning and store-manager support. Seven retail companies are slated to participate in phased demonstrations, with the planned official launch of the agents and execution platform in June 2027.

Reuters/Ipsos Poll Finds Many Voters Want Stronger AI Guardrails

PolicyPublic Opinion

A new Reuters/Ipsos poll found 57% of registered voters said the Trump administration was not taking AI risks seriously enough or at all; 54% said the same of Congress. The poll also found that 62% saw AI as capable of getting out of control and threatening humanity's future.

These findings point to a policy debate shaped not only by catastrophic-risk concerns but also jobs, health and safety, and local data-center impacts. They are survey responses—not evidence of a settled public consensus—and arrive ahead of the November midterms.

IMF Chief Flags AI Investment as a Source of Uneven Economic Pressure

EconomyPolicy

IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva warned that the AI investment boom is contributing to a positive demand shock and inflationary pressure even as high energy prices and public debt weigh on the global economy. She said the AI-driven gains are bypassing many countries.

The warning frames AI's macroeconomic effects as uneven: investment can accelerate demand and productivity expectations, while the benefits and costs are distributed differently across economies. It adds a global-policy lens to debates often focused on company-level adoption.
