Nvidia without its founder: Huang's shock resignation now reverberates into courtrooms and geopolitics — DOJ probes opened, first-ever earnings call Nov 17 under an interim CEO, and the scandal becoming a U.S.–China flashpoint

TOP STORYPolicyIndustry

The company's own release was brutally short: Jensen Huang — President, CEO and director since 1993 — resigned Thursday, November 12, effective immediately, no reason given. CFO Colette Kress, at Nvidia since 2013 with prior finance stints at Cisco and Microsoft, keeps her job while serving as interim CEO with no timeline for a successor, and longtime director Harvey C. Jones, former CEO of Synopsys, becomes the company's first-ever board chair — a governance post Nvidia never needed in 33 years. NPR filed the departure "amid spy scandal," a reference to the October 29 New York Times report tying Huang to an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer that U.S. officials suggested — never proven publicly — may work for Chinese intelligence, and to a second affair with a direct report reported November 11 that triggered a board investigation. Markets did the rest: a detailed read of the 8-K and tape puts the stock down more than 13% at Thursday's open, wiping out roughly half a trillion dollars in value — Bloomberg's tally via IT Home says as much as 14%, dragging AI-linked names — though Yahoo's coverage noted a bounce since, and Bloomberg's bottom line was that Wall Street remains unworried about the AI investment boom itself.

Today the story turned geopolitical. The Associated Press reports this morning that the scandal is heightening America's AI rivalry with China ahead of President Trump's visit — the chips Huang personally sold are the contested terrain. NineZenith's Saturday brief, citing Semafor, adds that the Justice Department has opened multiple investigations into Nvidia — an antitrust probe plus two inquiries involving Megaspeed and Super Micro — with no criminal charges indicated so far; the same brief flags two high-severity local privilege-escalation flaws in Nvidia's Linux GPU driver (NvRoot, CVE-2026-47591) that could let GPU renters break into cloud hosts. House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast says Huang could testify as soon as next week, subpoena notwithstanding. And Tuesday, November 17, Kress takes the podium for the first fiscal-Q3 earnings call in Nvidia history without Huang — at roughly 6% of the S&P 500, the succession question is now a market-structure question.

The agents get a wallet: OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet with AmEx, Mastercard and Visa so 'dots' can pay — with a biometric gate on every purchase — while Google points Gemini at Google Wallet, read-only

ProductAI AgentsCommerce

Reuters and CNBC report OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, letting its always-on "dots" agents complete online purchases on a user's behalf after explicit approval. The design is deliberately conservative: built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, it uses payment tokens so neither the agent nor the merchant ever sees the user's actual card details, requires a biometric check such as Face ID for every transaction, and preserves existing card rewards and protections. Access starts with U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18+ with eligible cards, extending to agents in ChatGPT Work and Codex; OpenAI says it is not taking a cut of transactions at launch — though CNBC notes the plumbing could seed a new revenue stream — and is publishing guidance so businesses can accept agent-led checkouts without breaking existing systems. Seeking Alpha adds the friction: some merchants may simply block agents.

Google's answer moves the money question one notch back. Museum of AI's Saturday brief and the Nov 14 news briefing report an optional U.S. link between the Gemini app and Google Wallet: tag @Google Wallet to review past spending, catch forgotten subscriptions, generate budget charts or plan holiday buys, with deeper insight by linking bank and card accounts via Plaid — strictly read-only: Gemini cannot move money, pay, or manage accounts, and the link is revocable at any time. Two philosophies of agentic finance shipped in 24 hours: an agent that spends under a biometric gate versus an assistant that only reads. The rails business agrees the spend era is coming: CX Network notes machine-readable loyalty-payments platform Imprint raised $130M (Khosla-led, with Thrive and Ribbit) explicitly positioning for agentic commerce.

Policy round-up: the White House floats a 12-month liability shield for AI incident disclosure, the EU publishes its sandbox rulebook, New York's frontier registry opens — and 40% of game developers now use generative AI

AI PolicyIndustry

AI Weekly, citing Bloomberg: at the Trump AI task force's second meeting on November 12, officials recommended federal agencies grant at least a year of regulatory flexibility to AI companies that voluntarily disclose cyber vulnerabilities or incidents in good faith, calling existing enforcement standards a "chilling effect" on information sharing; the non-binding guidance also backs a voluntary incident-reporting pipeline feeding defensive data back to industry and reauthorizing the 2015 Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act — without overriding public health or safety obligations. Central Intelligence's governance briefing covers the EU side: the Commission published common operating rules for AI regulatory sandboxes (adopted Nov 10) — every Member State must stand up at least one sandbox by August 2, 2027, complete applications assessed within three months, exit reports in 45 working days, recognized across borders but conferring no market-entry certification. The same briefing relays, with an unverified flag, that New York has opened registration ahead of its RAISE Act's January 1 start: frontier developers with >$500M annual revenue would register, publish safety frameworks and report critical incidents within 72 hours, at penalties up to $1M (first violation) and $3M after.

And the creative industries have voted: Agentdex's Saturday pulse cites GDC's November survey of 2,600+ game workers finding 40% now use generative AI at work, up from 36% last year — while GamesRadar reports Crimson Desert maker Pearl Abyss telling Q3 earnings shareholders it is implementing AI "throughout our entire development pipeline" — repetitive tasks, content support, test optimization — with core gameplay kept human-made and its creature-collector DokeV now targeted for late 2028. Adoption with an apology attached: the backlash-priced compromise is now the standard corporate sentence.
