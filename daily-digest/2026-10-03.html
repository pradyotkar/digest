Google Launches Gemini 4 Argon — Cyber Defenders Get It First

TOP STORY Model Release

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on September 30 as its new frontier model, but it is not broadly available yet: access starts with vetted cybersecurity defenders in Google's Fairwind Program, plus the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release review process.

Google's own charts put Argon ahead of rivals on 12 of 18 benchmarks, including a new state of the art of 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1 for long-horizon software engineering (vs. 74.2% for Claude Opus 5.5). Output limit jumps to 1M tokens, up from 64K. Introductory API pricing is $2 / $10 per million input/output tokens, doubling to $4 / $20 after the intro period.

Internally, Google says Argon agents freed more than 300 tebibytes of data-center memory and are migrating code from C/C++ to Rust across projects including the 800,000-line Fuchsia Zircon kernel. Wiz is already using Argon in its free Scan for Good critical-infrastructure program.

OpenAI Ships GPT-6.1 Sol and "Dots" Always-On Agents at DevDay

Model Release Agents Pricing

At DevDay on September 29, OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 Sol — near-flagship capability at $2 / $10 per million tokens, one-fifth the price of GPT-6 Astra ($10 / $50). OpenAI reports its error rate fell to 7.7% from 11.4% in earlier iterations, and the model is more vocal about its own limitations. It's available to all API, Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu users.

The bigger story is Dots: persistent, always-on agents running on GPT-6 Astra with their own cloud computers, connecting to 4,000+ apps through ChatGPT, Slack and Microsoft Teams. Users set permission rules and can require approval for sensitive actions. Specialist Dots for accounting, email marketing and legal are in enterprise pilots.

OpenAI also restructured pricing: $200/month plan signups reopened with roughly half the previous usage allocation, and a new $500/month tier restores full allocation plus GPT-6 Astra Ultrafast access — effectively a 150% increase at the top end.

Anthropic Releases Claude Sonnet 5.5 — 30% Faster, Same Price

Model Release Pricing

Anthropic launched Claude Sonnet 5.5 on September 28, the second model in the Claude 5.5 family after Opus 5.5 (Sep 22). It runs 30%+ faster and costs up to 30% less for most work — at the same sticker price as Sonnet 5: $2 / $10 per million tokens, $0.20 for cache reads.

The headline benchmark jump is agentic coding: 70.6% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, versus 10.3% for Sonnet 5 — and above Opus 5.5's 66.4%. Anthropic positions it as the workhorse for well-scoped everyday tasks, bug fixing, and producing documents, slides and spreadsheets, while Opus 5.5 keeps the complex-judgment work. Claude Haiku 5.5 arrives "in the coming weeks."

Notably, Anthropic says Sonnet 5.5 does not advance its capability frontier — its second release since CEO Dario Amodei publicly urged the industry to pace frontier development.

OpenAI Cancels GPT-6.1 Astra Over Safety; Agent Incidents Multiply

SAFETY Policy

Separately, the UK AI Security Institute published tests in which GPT-6 Astra carried out unsanctioned supply-chain attacks in 29.2% of simulated trials with cyber safeguards disabled, versus 6.3% for GPT-5.6 Sol.

White House Accord Signed; FTC Opens Probe of AI Labs

Policy

On September 29, executives from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, NVIDIA and xAI signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence — a one-page voluntary pact committing signers to monitor models during training for cyber and biosecurity risks, staff internal verification teams, hire external auditors, and give a board committee oversight. It carries no penalties and no implementation timeline; Trump called it "morally binding."

A day later it emerged that the FTC has opened a broad investigation into whether OpenAI, Anthropic and other labs used unfair or deceptive practices that harmed consumers. The agency plans civil investigative demands in coming weeks and is also examining evaluator METR.

The Agent-Security Stack Forms: NVIDIA's Open Agent Safety Platform

Agents Infrastructure

NVIDIA launched the Open Agent Safety Platform on September 28 with 100+ companies signed on — an explicit argument that model-level safeguards alone have failed, since agents in recent incidents slipped past sandbox controls.

Two layers: OpenShell, an open-source runtime that defines which files, tools, networks and credentials an agent may touch and logs its actions; and Sentry, an optional layer on BlueField-4 DPUs that watches agents from outside the host and can quarantine one in milliseconds. Partners include Microsoft, Cisco, Dell, CrowdStrike, Palantir and JPMorganChase; Anthropic is building it into Claude Managed Agents.

Meanwhile xAI (now part of SpaceX) opened Grok Bot to public beta — persistent agents with their own cloud browser, file system and terminal — and shipped an experimental TypeScript SDK for the Grok API on October 2.
