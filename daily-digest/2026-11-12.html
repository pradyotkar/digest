Proven, not promised: DeepMind's AlphaProof Code formally verifies Signal's protocol down to machine code — six million lines of Lean, 1% human review

ResearchSecurityLandmark

Wired reports Google DeepMind researchers used an agentic AI framework called AlphaProof Code to mathematically prove key cryptographic guarantees of the Signal protocol — a landmark for both cryptography and AI. The technical recap has the scale: agents generated over six million lines of Lean proof in four weeks and verified libsignal, Signal's reference implementation, at the machine-code level — forward secrecy, authentication, post-compromise security and handshake secrecy — with roughly 1% of the codebase flagged for a human look. No new vulnerabilities were found in the audited portions, but per the paper itself, when pointed at older Signal versions carrying known, long-fixed bugs, AlphaProof Code correctly identified them and refused to certify the protocol — the negative-control test that makes the positive result meaningful. DeepMind is releasing the specification and proof to seed further verification work.

Why it matters beyond the demo: formal verification is the missing compliance primitive. Australia's new regime (yesterday's story) contemplates independent evaluation of frontier models, and New York's RAISE Act — live below — mandates incident reporting and transparency. A framework that turns "we believe this code is secure" into a machine-checked proof, at 1% human cost, converts security claims from marketing into artifacts. The same 48 hours brought AlphaGenome 2, DeepMind's updated DNA model that, per GPTOnline's feed, predicts disease risk cell by cell by modeling how variants act in specific cell types — science and safety tooling arriving on the same news cycle.

New York is now open for business on the RAISE Act: registration portal live, frontier labs enroll by January 1 — or face up to $3M per violation

AI PolicyCompliance

TechCrunch reports New York opened its RAISE Act registration portal Tuesday: large frontier AI developers — companies above $500 million in annual revenue, which sweeps in OpenAI and Anthropic — must register by January 1, when the law's obligations bite. From that date, covered labs must publish safety frameworks on their websites, issue public model transparency reports, and report critical safety incidents within 72 hours, under penalties up to $1 million for a first violation and $3 million for subsequent ones. Enforcement sits with the newly formed Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity, and Trust (DIGIT) at the Department of Financial Services, and cisonews.ai's tracker notes the Act's definitional boundary: enterprises that merely build on other companies' models are outside the frontier-developer scope — though they'll be reading their vendors' published frameworks in vendor-risk reviews.

"We're building a national model for smart, sensible AI regulation," Hochul said, per Silicon Report.
