Two proofs, one weekend: Buckmaster, Alpöge and Coiculescu claim finite-time blowup for the unforced 3D Euler and Navier-Stokes equations — "with heavy AI assistance," Lean-formalized across ~2,270 files and ~600,000 lines — closing the forcing loophole critics found in OpenAI's September solution, though the numerical certificate the result depends on is still withheld (only its hash is published)

TOP STORYResearchModels

The news Scientific American (Nov 20) framed as the resolution of the September rivalry landed the same morning — November 20 — as Alpöge's Riemann note, and the weekend coverage finally made sense of it. CellCog's reconstruction (Nov 20–22) has the record: Terence Tao wrote on Nov 20 that Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster and Matei Coiculescu, "with heavy AI assistance," produced a human-readable proof of finite-time blowup for the unforced incompressible 3D Euler equations, formalized in Lean, plus a second paper claiming the same for unforced incompressible Navier-Stokes — the 79-page Navier-Stokes write-up being, in Tao's words, "technically human-readable" but still needing "a proper, professionally written proof." OfficeChai (Nov 21) details the architecture: a conditional theorem — if a smooth backward discretely self-similar solution exists, blowup follows, with the gradient exploding at the Leray (T−t)^(−1/4) rate — plus a computer-assisted proof that the self-similar solution exists, checked by rigorous interval arithmetic at 128-bit precision with the Arb library. The GitHub repositories went public Nov 19 (Euler) and Nov 20 at 10:40 UTC (Navier-Stokes), a ~600,000-line, 2,270-file Lean 4 library built on Mathlib, standard axioms only. Buckmaster's framing of the rush job: "After last time, we couldn't risk waiting."

The stakes are the September grudge. OpenAI's Sept 8 proof — ~100 agents for 50 hours on Euler, ~10,000 agents and 88 hours on Navier-Stokes — used a smooth external force, which satisfies the letter of Clay's problem statement but struck many experts as a loophole; Buckmaster alleged the company scooped his and Alpöge's program (OpenAI denied foul play, saying its result was unforced where theirs was forced, per Quanta). These new results need no force at all — the distinguishing line SciAm calls "the 'real' Navier-Stokes problem." The caveats are equally load-bearing: the numerical certificate is not in the repository — only its cryptographic hash, with release promised "soon" — the interval-arithmetic verification is not formalized in Lean, and the Lean code was patched on the day of release (OfficeChai). Clay has not spoken, and neither side has claimed the prize changes hands; yesterday's Riemann verifier, MIT's Andrew Sutherland, has yet to publish a comparator run on this code.

ApplicationsModelsChips

The technical report, "Advancing chip design with our models" (openai.com, dated Nov 18, coverage rolling through Nov 22 via AIToolsRecap), is the first with numbers: up to 14% aggregate PPA improvement on blocks of AMD's flagship GPU IP, measured against heavily optimized baselines; AMD has folded some model-written changes into a design already sent for manufacturing and staged others for future production files. Wccftech details the method: this is not ML-accelerated floorplanning, the decades-old corner of EDA — the models rewrote register-transfer-level code itself, reworking adders, multipliers, format conversions and reduction logic, moving computation across pipeline stages to share hardware and drop intermediate storage, with Synopsys tools confirming each rewrite preserved behavior. AMD also verified single-digit PPA gains across key blocks of its upcoming next-gen flagship CPU from model-written designs, though neither company named the products (HWBusters). On OpenAI's own Jalapeño inference chip the claims are silicon-proven at bring-up: ~13% standard-cell area cut in the vector unit, ~10% in the matrix unit, one multiplier redesign saving ~15% area while halving latency from two cycles to one; work began with GPT-4o-era models in early 2025, and OpenAI plans a dedicated model for operating Synopsys's toolchain. Silicon Noon's deep dive carries the best check on the enthusiasm: OpenAI hardware chief Richard Ho said last month he still does not fully trust AI design changes, so every one is human-checked — "that is not a hedge; it is the operating model." Every figure is OpenAI's; AMD has published none (AIToolsRecap, Nov 22).

The inference supply chain has a leak problem: Together AI says an AI coding agent committed the credential that let an intruder download June–July prompt samples (MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2, Kimi K2.6) from a private Hugging Face repo — while Dragos chief research officer HD Moore's ~2 million canary-token prompts found at least three AI hosting providers compromised and zero-data-retention flags silently ignored

SECURITYAI AgentsGovernance

Shattered.io's reconstruction (Nov 22) of Together AI's Nov 21 security notice lays out the chain in five steps: engineers were developing xorl, a public GitHub repository; an AI coding agent working the project committed a credential that granted access to a private Hugging Face repository holding stored prompt samples from June and July 2026; an unauthorized party obtained the key and downloaded the samples before automated scanning flagged the exposure and the commit was reverted. The affected traffic: requests to three models — MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 — mainly from four sample days between June 24 and July 14. The notice discloses roughly a four-month gap between the last affected sample day and customer notification, and does not say how long the credential stayed public; OpenRouter separately warned customers that MiniMax prompts may have been read and pulled its ZDR labels (per ExplainX's Nov 22 trend board). The framing that stings: "the developer was software" — agents inherit the developer's whole environment including every token in the shell, can push without a per-commit human gate, and the secrets they touch are usually broad-scoped; any one of those three fixes would have changed the outcome.

The same weekend, Crypto Briefing via AI Weekly (Nov 22) reported the results of the largest field audit of inference hygiene to date: Dragos CRO HD Moore ran ~2 million canary-token prompts across AI inference services from July through November and found at least three AI hosting providers compromised, resellers silently logging prompts, and providers ignoring zero-data-retention and no-data-collusion flags. And the Cloud Security Alliance labs desk (Nov 22) covered NebuSec's Nov 20 disclosure of a three-stage exploit chain against Meta's Muse personal agent: a bundled-Chromium renderer bug yielded a shell inside the agent container, then the browser was used to exfiltrate conversation history and stored memories past the Sentinel egress gate without approval. Together with RunPod's withdrawal (Nov 22, per Pillitteri's index) of an MCP order that told agents to hide a survey from users, the weekend's throughline is governance, not capability: the agent tier is now an active attack surface, and "ZDR" is starting to mean "claimed."
