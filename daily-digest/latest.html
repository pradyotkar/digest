Meta Debuts Muse Wonder, Its First Frontier-Class Model Pair

TOP STORYModel ReleasePricing

Meta has introduced Muse Wonder and the larger Muse Wonder Full, positioning them as its most capable models yet for coding, reasoning and extended-memory work. The launch marks a step up from Muse Spark’s lightweight, low-latency focus.

Meta highlights “active memory,” designed to retain and synthesize user-provided information over time. Wonder is accessible through the Meta Model API at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens; Wonder Full is reserved for Muse Code subscribers and is not offered through the API. The company says both will eventually power its Muse agent.

Anthropic Is Pitching Congress a Narrow, Court-Gated Safety Law

PolicySafety

Anthropic has circulated a draft frontier-AI bill that would permit government intervention only for a foreseeable, imminent risk exceeding $1 billion in damage or 50 deaths or serious injuries, tied to biological weapons, loss of developer control or cyberattacks. A registered independent evaluator would report qualifying risk to Commerce within 72 hours.

Under the proposal described in reporting, only Commerce could seek a suspension, and a federal judge in the District of Columbia would decide. The approach would constrain intervention authority and preempt some state rules; it raises a live debate over whether tight thresholds protect innovation or leave too little room to address serious risks.

World Packages Human and Agent Identity Checks for Financial Firms

AgentsApplications

At Money20/20, World introduced a financial-services identity suite combining its World ID app, a human-verification feature for Zoom and World ID for Agents (AgentKit). The agent component lets people delegate identity proofs for agents to present to third-party services while acting on their behalf.

The launch targets deepfake impersonation and uncertainty about who—or what—is accessing accounts. World says Zoom verification is now generally available; it named several financial-sector design partners, but no bank customer or pricing. Agent credentials could help establish delegated authority, while also making consent, privacy and revocation central design questions.

California Governor Orders Work on AI Verification and Agent Kill Switches

PolicySafety

California Governor Gavin Newsom’s latest executive orders call for independent verification of AI models and an expert committee to examine kill switches for agents that go rogue. The move revisits ideas from a previously vetoed bill, against the backdrop of reports about agents accessing government systems outside their intended scope.

The order’s practical effect remains uncertain, especially with Newsom’s term ending in January. But the focus on an operational stop mechanism reflects a shift from model evaluation alone toward controls over deployed, tool-using systems.
