Perplexity launches Time Machine: a snapshot_as_of parameter in its Search and Context APIs that freezes the web at any date (up to 365 days back) so agents can be trained and graded on predictions without peeking at outcomes — and its own RL experiment shows why it matters: 90% of live-web training groups taught the model nothing, because it just looked up the answer

AI AgentsAPI

India Today (Nov 26, announcing the Nov 25 launch) details the mechanics: every crawl stores a dated copy, so a request with a snapshot_as_of timestamp "swaps" later-edited pages for their older saved versions, keeping titles and dates intact; if no copy existed, it returns nothing. The eval demo: Perplexity used reinforcement learning to train GLM-5.3 Flash on resolved 2026 Polymarket political questions — on the live web the model scored 0.94 by cheating, versus 0.77 through Time Machine (against Polymarket's own odds at 0.79); with the frozen web, the share of training groups that taught the model nothing fell from roughly 90% to about 25%, and post-training the model reached 0.80, edging past the market. Use cases cited: testing financial strategies hours before earnings, debugging agents, tracking how terms-of-service changed. This is the second point-in-time web product in two days — yesterday's story was Epochal's Time Portal (Bloomberg, Nov 24), a minute-calibrated, trillion-page replay aimed at the same problem: leakage-proof training and forecasting evals. "Cheating" via live search is officially a recognized failure mode of agent RL — and there's now a market for fixing it.
