AI AgentsNEW LAB

AI Weekly's roundup (Nov 24–25) of Bloomberg's Nov 24 report: Guodong Zhang, Zihang Dai and Manuel Kroiss — founding members who left xAI after its February 2026 merger with SpaceX — publicly launched Epochal on Nov 24, joined by Alex Bocharoff and Frank Luan, staffed from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind. The pitch is "superhuman prediction," trained "through decisions and observed outcomes rather than static hindsight-filled text" — every decision is a prediction. The Time Portal sandbox, built in 90 days, lets agents browse the web frozen at specific past dates, "calibrated to the minute to prevent information leakage into simulations", per Unite.AI, which relays the 0.98-vs-0.16-vs-0.49 correlation table from Epochal's own 347-question test set. Two honest caveats sit in the coverage: the funding source beyond Zhang's personal $100M is undisclosed, the trillion-page replay's sourcing/licensing is unexplained, and the headline 0.98 is an internal result on an internal benchmark. Still, methodologically this is the interesting direction for evals — point-in-time environments where an agent's forecasts can be scored against outcomes without contamination. In an ecosystem that just heard (Story 1) that autonomous agents can game their monitors, Epochal's bet is that a closed, replayable world makes the games measurable.

ApplicationsHealthcare

DeepHealth's GlobeNewswire release (Nov 24) confirms the 510(k) (file K262709, cleared Nov 23): the RadNet subsidiary's Mammo Risk works from already-completed mammograms, flags elevated future risk in patients showing no current signs of cancer, and — per TNW — performed consistently across subgroups including Black women and women with dense breasts, both historically underserved by traditional risk models like Tyrer-Cuzick. The systemic bet on screening AI grows apace: per Medical Xpress via AFP (Nov 23), England's NHS launched the world's largest AI-aided breast-cancer screening trial — ~700,000 women over three years, £10M, led by Warwick and Cambridge, with Health Secretary Yvette Cooper promising "proper safeguards." And evidence keeps stacking in adjacent clinical work: an Imperial College study in JAMA Network Open (via Medical Xpress, Nov 18) found 92% of AI-generated discharge summaries clinically acceptable vs 70% clinician-written in blinded review of 1,294 summaries.
